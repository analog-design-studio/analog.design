Summed for iPhone · iOS 26 and later

Summed turns a photo of text into a short English summary. Point the camera at a sign, a menu, a page or a piece of mail, tap the white dot, and get a title, a summary of one to three sentences and a category. Save the ones you want to a library you can browse as a list, on a map or on a calendar. The reading and summarizing happen on your iPhone. No account.

A summary is a shortcut, not a substitute. It is written by a machine, and it can leave things out, misread them, mistranslate them or get them wrong. For anything that matters, read the original.

Requirements: iPhone with iOS 26 or later. Summaries need Apple Intelligence turned on, which means iPhone 15 Pro or later. Without it, Summed shows the text as it was read. The price is shown on Summed's App Store page.

Support

Read this first

  • A summary is written by a machine. It can leave out the part that matters, misread a word or a number, or state something the text never said.
  • Never rely on a summary for medicine or dosage labels, allergens or ingredients, safety warnings and instructions, legal or official mail, contracts, forms, bills, deadlines or amounts of money. Read the original, and ask a qualified person when it matters.
  • A summary of text in another language is a machine translation. It is not a certified or checked translation.
  • Summed reads only the start of long text. Anything past roughly two dense pages may not be in the summary at all. A note under the summary tells you when this has happened.
  • Summed doesn't keep the original text or the full photo. Keep the original of anything you need.
  • Don't use Summed while driving, or anywhere your attention is needed.

Taking a summary

  • The first time you open Summed, a "Before you begin" screen asks you to accept the terms and privacy policy. The camera and location requests come after that. From then on Summed opens on the camera. Point it at the text and tap the white dot.
  • You'll see "Analyzing. Hold please." while your iPhone reads the text and writes the summary.
  • The summary page shows a title, the summary and a suggested category. Notes directly under the summary say that it is machine-made, whether it is a machine translation and from which language, and whether it covers only the first part of a long text.
  • Tap the plus at the top right to add it to your library. Nothing is saved until you do. The plus turns into a check mark once it's saved.
  • Tap the lens at the top left to go back to the camera. A summary you didn't add is discarded.

Getting a good read

  • Hold the phone steady, fill the frame with the text, and keep the page flat and evenly lit.
  • Take one page or one sign at a time. For a long document, take it in sections.
  • "No text found. Try moving closer." means the camera couldn't make out any text.
  • Handwriting, tiny print, glare, curved pages, columns and tables are the most likely to be misread.

Summaries and Apple Intelligence

  • Summaries are written by Apple's on-device model, so Apple Intelligence must be turned on in Settings. It needs iPhone 15 Pro or later.
  • When Apple Intelligence isn't available, Summed shows the first part of the text as it was read, about 280 characters, with a line saying it isn't a summary.
  • Summaries are always in English.
  • Summed reads about the first 6,000 characters of text in Latin letters, and about 1,800 in other scripts such as Chinese, Japanese, Arabic or Cyrillic. If Apple's model can't take that much, Summed tries again with half, and then a quarter.
  • The same photo can give a different summary on another day or after an iOS update.

Categories

  • Summed suggests one category: books, magazines, posters, signs, menus, manuals, mail, labels, receipts, forms, notes or other. Tap another to change it.
  • Tap the plus at the end of the row to add your own category, up to 24 characters.

Library

  • Tap the book at the bottom right to open the library. Switch between List, Map and Cal at the top.
  • Filter by category, by language and, when location is on, by city.
  • Tap a snippet to open it. Tap its title to edit it. Tap the photo to see all of it, and tap again to crop it back.
  • To rename from the list, swipe right and tap Rename, or press and hold.
  • To share, swipe right and tap Share, or open the snippet and tap the paper airplane. A share sends the title, the summary and its notes as text. It doesn't send the photo or the place.
  • To delete, swipe left and confirm. An Undo bar shows for six seconds. After that, or once you close the library or leave Summed, the snippet is gone for good.
  • The summary text itself can't be edited in this version.

Map and places

  • With location on, Summed saves where each snippet was captured, shows its city in the list and pins it on the map.
  • Place names need an internet connection at the moment you save. A snippet saved offline keeps its pin but gets no place name.
  • If you decline location, snippets are saved without a place, and the map shows a message with a link to Settings.
  • If you turn location off later, places and the map are hidden. The places already saved stay with their snippets until you delete those snippets.

Calendar

  • The calendar starts with the month you first used Summed. Each dot is a snippet. Tap a day to list its snippets under that week.

What is saved

  • For each snippet you add: the title, summary, category, language, date and time, the place if location is on, and a smaller black-and-white copy of the photo.
  • Not saved: the full-color photo, the full text that was read, and anything you didn't add to the library.
  • Summed doesn't add anything to your Photos library.

Your data

  • Everything is stored only on your iPhone and is included in its iCloud or computer backup. There is no account, no sync between devices, no search and no export in this version.
  • If you delete Summed, everything in it is deleted too. We have no copy and can't recover it.

Troubleshooting

  • "Summed needs the camera to read text." Tap Open Settings and turn on Camera for Summed.
  • I get the text as read, not a summary. Apple Intelligence is off, still downloading, or not available on your iPhone. Turn it on in Settings and try again later.
  • The summary is wrong or misses something. Move closer and take the text in smaller sections. Then read the original. No retake makes a summary safe to rely on.
  • "Language not detected". There was too little text, or it mixed languages. The summary may still be right. The note under the summary then says it is a machine translation if the original wasn't in English.
  • The map is blank or a place name is missing. Maps and place names need an internet connection.
